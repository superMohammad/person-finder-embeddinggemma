(c) how to read it
a photo, placed by its 768 numbers a description line to the centre of its person
768 numbers cannot be drawn, so they are squashed to 2 with PCA, which keeps the two directions where the points differ most. Short lines mean a description already lands next to the right person.
(d) the gap that does the work
Average cosine score of a description with photos of its own person and with photos of other people. Only the distance between the two matters.
Figure 3. Each panel shows real model outputs for six test-set people that the model never trained on. (a) Out of the box, photos group with photos and sentences with sentences (the well-known “modality gap”), and every sentence is about equally close to everyone. (b) After contrastive fine-tuning, the descriptions land on top of the photos they describe. People dressed alike, such as the two in red, share a neighbourhood, but within it each sentence still puts its own person first: all 60 sentences here. (d) The gap between “own” and “other” grows from 0.07 to 0.39. That gap is what lets one threshold, 0.45, separate the right person from the crowd in video.
