Method · one figure, seven steps

One model, two uses, one green box.

The whole project in one figure and seven short steps, from the dataset to a live mall camera. Each step says what was done, and why it was done that way.

01Method 02Search & grades 03Training details 04Live video 05A real crowd 06Results 07How it compares Read the full explanationPDF · plain words
01

One model reads words and photos.

EmbeddingGemma-2 sends a sentence, or a photo through its vision tower, into the same Gemma backbone, and ends with 768 numbers. Training pulls each description onto its photo. In video, every detected person becomes a photo, and the best match gets the box.

02

Search is one product and a sort.

Gallery photos are encoded once and cached. A query is encoded in milliseconds and scored against every photo with one matrix–vector product. Grades are averaged over 2,000 test queries on 200 people the model never saw.

WhenPhotos are encoded once; queries are encoded live.
Why cacheEncoding 20,505 photos takes about 15 minutes, while one sentence takes milliseconds.
Why several gradesRank-1 checks the first guess, and mAP and mINP check all of that person's photos.
03

Three training details that matter.

Batches never hold the same person twice, so a correct match is never punished. A cached loss gives 128 negatives at the memory cost of 16. Matryoshka training keeps a 256-number vector almost as good as the full 768.

WhenAfter the zero-shot baseline was measured.
Why lr 2e-5Small steps learn this task without forgetting everything else.
Why 3 epochsValidation stopped improving after the first one.
04

Video: smooth over time, then gate.

Each track is re-encoded every 10 frames and averaged with an EMA. Every frame, the best track wins only if it scores at least 0.45 and its box is full-body shaped, so strangers and half-visible people never get the box.

05

Graded against 62,315 labelled heads.

On the Mall dataset, each labelled head may match one person box, and only if it sits in the top 45% of that box. The detector is precise (97.7%), but at 640 px it finds only 39% of a dense crowd, and 66% at 1280 px.

06

40% → 71% first-guess accuracy.

The same test, the same code, 200 people the model never trained on. Every grade nearly doubles, and the right person is in the top 10 for 93% of searches.

07

Against CLIP, IRRA, RDE, RaSa, APTM.

On RSTPReid's long descriptions, the fine-tuned model beats every listed method. In the Mall video, where the descriptions are short colour cues, zero-shot CLIP nearly catches up and runs 10× faster. The patterned "striped shirt" is where fine-tuning still wins clearly.
