One model, two uses, one green box.

The whole project in six figures, from the dataset to a live mall camera. Each step says what was done, and why it was done that way.

01

A sentence and a photo become the same kind of thing.

EmbeddingGemma-2 turns text and images into 768 numbers. If the numbers point the same way, the sentence describes the photo. Search ranks photos by that score, and video does the same with people cut out of each frame.

02

Graded on strangers only.

RSTPReid has 4,101 people, 20,505 photos and two sentences per photo. Train, val and test share no person, so the test score measures how well the model finds people it has never seen.

WhenBefore any code was written.
Why by personA shared person would let the model remember a face instead of reading the words.
Why many gradesRank-1 checks the first guess, and mAP checks whether all of that person's photos were found.
03

Fine-tuning opens a gap.

Before training, a sentence was almost as close to strangers (0.66) as to its own person (0.73). After training, the gap is 0.12 against 0.51. That gap is what makes a single threshold work in video.

04

Match the pairs, push the rest away.

Each step is a 128-way multiple-choice test, taken in both directions. Batches never hold two photos of the same person, so a right answer is never marked wrong. Matryoshka training keeps even a 256-number vector useful.

WhenAfter the zero-shot baseline was measured.
Why lr 2e-5Small steps learn this task without forgetting everything else.
Why 3 epochsValidation stopped improving after the first one.
05

Video is a stream of person photos.

YOLO finds people, ByteTrack keeps their ids, and each crop is re-encoded every 10 frames and averaged over time. The best person wins only with a score of at least 0.45 and a full-body box shape.

06

40% → 71% first-guess accuracy.

The same test, the same code, 200 people the model never trained on. Every grade nearly doubles, and the right person is in the top 10 for 93% of searches.

07

Out in a real mall.

Mall dataset: 2,000 frames and 62,315 hand-marked heads. The language side transfers, scoring each described shopper 0.60–0.72. The detector is the limit: at 640 px it finds 39% of people in the crowd, and at 1280 px it finds 66%.
